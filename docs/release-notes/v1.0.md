The first release of Gundil Music: a YouTube Music style player you host yourself, with background play and Bluetooth speakers. No subscription needed.

## What you get

- **Laid out like YouTube Music** on phone and desktop:
  - Home: mood chips, Quick picks, Listen again.
  - Explore: new releases, charts by country, moods & genres.
  - Search: suggestions, a top result, and filters for songs, videos, albums, artists and playlists.
  - Album, artist and playlist pages.
- **Now playing**: Up next with autoplay radio, Related, shuffle and repeat.
- **Lyrics that follow the song.**
  - The current line lights up and stays in the middle. Dots fill in during the intro and instrumental breaks, and tapping a line jumps there.
  - Scroll to read ahead, and **Back to current line** brings you back.
  - The full-screen view shows big, centred lyrics over the cover's colours.
  - When YouTube Music has no timed lyrics, the server gets them from LRCLIB, a free open lyrics database, for a song of the same length.
- **Song / Video switch.** Flip to Video and the real music video plays, in step with the song, with a full-screen button. Songs that have a music video switch to it, and other songs keep their album art. Pick 360p to 1080p in Settings. When you minimise the app the picture stops but the music keeps going. Video plays on the phone or computer you're holding. A headless server has no screen, so it plays audio only.
- **Offline downloads.** Download songs, albums and playlists to your phone (or computer) and play them with no connection. Use ⋮ → Download, or the download button next to Play. Away from home or with the server off, the app opens straight to your downloads. Find them, and remove them, in Library → Downloads.
- **Crossfade** (Settings): fade each song into the next over 2–12 seconds, on the device you're using.
- **Drag to reorder** the Up next queue and your own playlists, with the mouse or a finger on the ≡ handle.
- **Touch gestures on phones.** Swipe the mini player up to open the player, and swipe the player down to close it. Swipe left or right on the mini player or the cover to change song. Swipe the Up next / Lyrics tabs up and down, pull a menu down to close it, and pull down at the top of a page to load it again.
- **Steadier with a slow or flaky YouTube.** Pages you've opened before show at once, even after a restart, while a fresh copy loads behind the scenes. Brief network or YouTube errors are retried automatically, and if YouTube still fails you get the last copy (up to a week old) instead of an error. Home and Explore load when the server starts.
- **Sleep timer** (timer button in the player, or the ⋮ menu): stop after 5, 10, 15, 30 or 45 minutes, an hour, or at the end of the song. The music fades out. It works on the phone and when the server plays to a speaker, and moves with the music if you switch between them.
- **Settings** (avatar menu):
  - audio quality for this device (Low uses less mobile data), video quality and autoplay;
  - for the server: region and language for YouTube Music results, speaker audio quality, and daily YouTube updates on or off;
  - "Update now", and clearing watch or search history.
- **Plays in the background, free.** Music keeps playing when you minimise the app, switch apps, swipe it away from Recents or turn the screen off, and the next song starts on its own. Open the app again and it's still there, playing. The Android app also pauses when headphones or a Bluetooth speaker disconnect, so music doesn't suddenly play from the phone speaker.
- **Speakers & Bluetooth** (cast button): play on the phone in your hand or on the server. A Linux server (such as a headless Raspberry Pi or Orange Pi) can scan, pair and connect Bluetooth speakers itself, and they reconnect after a reboot.
- **Your library on your server**: thumbs up/down, Liked Music, your own playlists, saved albums and playlists, subscribed artists, history and recent searches. No Google account needed.
- **Accounts (optional).** Turn them on in **Settings → Accounts** and each person signs in with their own name and password, and gets their own library: playlists, Liked Music, saved albums and artists, history and searches. The first account is the admin and keeps the library used so far. Admins add people, reset passwords and change server settings. To let people create their own account from the sign-in screen, turn on **Settings → Accounts → Let people create their own account**, for anyone on your network or only people with an invite code. If accounts aren't set up yet, it first makes yours the admin account. Phones and browsers stay signed in.
- **Import playlists.**
  - Paste a YouTube Music or YouTube playlist or album link into Search, or use **Library → Import playlist**. Every song is copied into a playlist of your own that you can edit and reorder.
  - In the app, any YouTube playlist or album's ⋮ menu has **Import as my playlist**.
- **Link your YouTube Music account** (Settings) to import your private playlists and Liked Music. It signs in with a cookies.txt export from your browser, per person.
- **Gets past "Sign in to confirm you're not a bot" by itself where it can.**
  - The server first retries as other YouTube players.
  - If that fails, it plays the song signed in with a linked YouTube Music account: yours first, then an admin's.
- **Server or client on every computer.** The Windows, Linux desktop, AppImage, Raspberry Pi and Orange Pi builds can each be the server, or a client of another Gundil Music server on your network. For example, a Windows PC can play your library from the Pi. Switch in **Settings → This computer → Change**, where you can type the server's address or let it find servers on your network, or run `gundil-music -connect 192.168.1.20`. A client needs no Python and fetches nothing from YouTube itself.
- **Quit Gundil Music** (avatar menu, on the computer running it). Closing the browser window leaves Gundil Music running for your other devices; starting it again opens the window of the one already running.
- **Android app**: finds Gundil Music servers on your Wi-Fi, with a media notification, lock-screen controls and Bluetooth headset buttons.
- **Windows and Linux desktop downloads that need nothing else installed.** Python, ytmusicapi, yt-dlp and Deno are bundled.
- **YouTube support updates itself.** The server checks once a day for new yt-dlp and ytmusicapi releases and installs them, including for the AppImage and the Windows zip. To update right away, use **Update** in Settings (or About) in the app, or run the server with `-update-youtube`. A failed update keeps the working version. Nothing needs restarting. Songs that are playing carry on, and open apps reload a page or retry a song that failed before the update.

The AppImage's start-menu shortcut is now just "Gundil Music", without the version. If your menu still shows "Gundil Music (v1.0)", remove the old shortcut (or let your AppImage integration tool remove it) and integrate the new AppImage. This build keeps the Android app clear of the status bar and gesture bar on Android 15, where the clock and icons used to sit over the top of the app. It also fixes the Android app closing as soon as a song started on the phone. The playback notification's icon was malformed, and Android killed the app over it. Every build is now tested in an Android emulator. The test plays a song, minimises the app, turns the screen off and swipes the app away from Recents, checking the music never stops. Then it downloads a song, switches the server off and plays the song offline.

`install.sh` sets up everything a board needs: mpv, BlueZ, PipeWire, Python with ytmusicapi and yt-dlp, and a JavaScript runtime. It also starts Gundil Music at boot. `SHA256SUMS.txt` has checksums for every file.

### How the pieces fit

Gundil Music is a server plus a player. The server streams from YouTube Music and keeps your library. You open the player on any device:

- **Headless Pi or Orange Pi:** the board is the server. Phones use the Android app, and computers use a browser. Connect a Bluetooth speaker from the cast button and the board plays through it, whatever your phone is doing.
- **Windows or Linux desktop:** by default the PC is the server and opens the player in your browser. Phones on the same Wi-Fi can use it too. Or make it a client of another server (Settings → This computer), and it plays that server's library.
- **Headless Pi or Orange Pi as a client:** `gundil-music -connect <server>` makes the board pass everything on to another Gundil Music server.
- **Android app:** the player for a server on your network. It plays on the phone (and its Bluetooth headphones) or remote-controls what the server plays.

The three APKs contain the same app: it has no native code. ARM64 is right for nearly every current phone.

## Known limitations

- **Installing over an earlier v1.0 APK.** Earlier v1.0 builds were signed with a different key, so Android won't install this one over them. Uninstall the old Gundil Music once, then install this one. This build is signed with Gundil Music's permanent release key, so later versions install straight over it. Your library is on the server, so nothing is lost. Your downloads are on the phone and go with the uninstall, so download them again afterwards.
- **Unofficial YouTube access.** Gundil Music uses YouTube Music's unofficial web API (ytmusicapi) and yt-dlp. It isn't affiliated with YouTube or Google, and using YouTube this way may be against YouTube's Terms of Service. It's meant for personal use.
- **YouTube's sign-in check.** YouTube sometimes asks a server to "sign in to confirm you're not a bot". This is common on cloud servers and VPNs and rare at home. Gundil Music retries by itself, and once a YouTube Music account is linked in Settings it plays signed in with that account. No app can switch the check off for good. In this release's automated tests on GitHub's servers, YouTube Music search worked but streams got this check.
- **No "Sign in with Google".** Google only offers it to apps on a registered public https address, which a home server isn't. Gundil Music accounts use a name and password, and YouTube Music accounts are linked with browser cookies, which you export again if YouTube signs them out.
- **YouTube changes often.** The daily auto-update covers this. If a song won't play in between, press **Update YouTube support** in About.
- **Raspberry Pi 1 and Pi Zero** (ARMv6) aren't supported.
- **In-app Bluetooth pairing works on Linux servers.** On Windows and Android, pair in the system settings.
- **Recommendations aren't personalised.** Home, mixes and radio come from YouTube Music without your account, even with one linked. A linked account is used for importing your playlists and for the sign-in check.
- **Not in this release:** podcasts and Chromecast.
- **Downloads stay on the device that downloaded them**, and a song's cover is only kept when the image host allows it. Otherwise the cover is missing while offline.
- **A computer's browser only opens the app offline from `localhost` or an `https://` address.** Over plain `http://` on your network, downloads play while the page is open. The Android app opens offline either way.
- **No login until you set up accounts.** Until then, and without `-auth`, anyone on your network can use the player. Don't open the port to the internet: it is plain HTTP.
